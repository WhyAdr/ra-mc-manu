The run is technically sound, but I would not interpret it as a clean 15-genome *M. cosmeticum* species pangenome yet. Biologically, it looks like a mixture of several near-clonal lineages, one major taxonomic outlier, and strict-mode filtering that removed genuine mobile-element biology.

## The headline numbers are misleading

| Dataset                               | Genomes | Pangenome union | Universal core | Core/union |
| ------------------------------------- | ------: | --------------: | -------------: | ---------: |
| Current analysis                      |      15 |          10,087 |          1,466 |      14.5% |
| Excluding `GCA_016197365.1`           |      14 |           9,149 |          5,295 |      57.9% |
| Your eight new isolates only          |       8 |           6,397 |          6,299 |      98.5% |
| Six public genomes, excluding outlier |       6 |           8,586 |          5,477 |      63.8% |

The jump from 1,466 to 5,295 universal families after removing one accession is enormous. It means the apparent tiny core is driven primarily by `GCA_016197365.1`, not by ordinary *M. cosmeticum* accessory diversity.  

Also, with 15 genomes:

* 15/15 = 100%
* 14/15 = 93.3%

Therefore the reported “soft core, 95–99%” category must be zero, and “core, ≥99%” means strictly 15/15. The default labels conceal the discrete sample-size effect.

The occupancy spectrum is almost a barcode for the sampled lineages:

|  Occupancy | Families | Main explanation                                     |
| ---------: | -------: | ---------------------------------------------------- |
|   1 genome |    1,142 | 938 belong only to `GCA_016197365.1`                 |
|  2 genomes |    2,183 | 2,163 belong to three closely paired public lineages |
|  8 genomes |      472 | Present in all eight new isolates only               |
| 14 genomes |    3,859 | 3,829 are absent only from `GCA_016197365.1`         |
| 15 genomes |    1,466 | Strict universal core                                |

So this is not a smooth continuum of rare genes characteristic of a well-sampled open pangenome. It is mainly a collection of lineage-specific blocks.

## `GCA_016197365.1` is almost certainly not comparable as-is

This accession is the dominant problem:

* 5,271 CDS records entered Panaroo.
* Only 2,458 CDS assignments survived into the final matrix: 46.6% retention.
* 2,813 CDSs were removed during graph cleaning.
* It retains 938 singleton families.
* Its full gene-content Jaccard similarity to every other genome is only approximately 0.18–0.21.
* It shares only about 1,480–1,511 families with any other genome.

Even conserved housekeeping orthologues were split into separate Panaroo families. Diagnostic protein alignments between ANT01 and `GCA_016197365.1` gave approximately:

| Protein | Protein identity |
| ------- | ---------------: |
| RecA    |            97.1% |
| RpsB    |            95.6% |
| NusA    |            95.2% |
| RbfA    |            92.0% |
| DapA    |            90.3% |

Thus genes such as `recA`, `rpsB`, `nusA`, and `dapA` are not genuinely absent; their divergent homologues were assigned to separate families. That is a classic signature of an accession outside the intended species boundary—or at minimum a very deep lineage that should not be mixed into a simple strain-level pangenome.

My recommendation is to quarantine this accession until you have checked:

* ANI and aligned fraction against the *M. cosmeticum* type/reference genome and your isolates;
* GTDB-Tk classification;
* CheckM2 completeness/contamination;
* GUNC or another chimerism check.

If ANI falls below the usual species-scale range or aligned fraction is poor, remove it from the main analysis and discuss it separately.

## Your eight isolates are essentially one very tight clonal complex

Across ANT/BODY/BULB/DESC:

* 6,299 of 6,397 families are universal.
* Only 98 families vary anywhere within the eight-genome cohort.
* Pairwise family-content Jaccard values are roughly 0.988–0.9995.
* Pooled nucleotide identity across equal-length, single-copy matched CDSs is approximately 99.959–99.99997%.

That last metric is not formal ANI, but it confirms extraordinarily close relationships. Some pairs differ by only a few family calls:

* ANT01–DESC01: 3-family symmetric difference.
* BODY01–DESC06: 4 families.
* BODY06–BULB06: 6 families.

This is good if your objective is fine-scale epidemiology or within-lineage mobile-element comparison, but it provides very little independent information about species-wide pangenome openness. For those eight, core-SNP analysis will be far more informative than a standard gene-content tree.

The public genomes also contain near-duplicates:

* `GCF_039668265.1` and `GCF_039668325.1` have identical binary family profiles: 7,358/7,358 families.
* Their equal-length matched CDS nucleotide sequences were 100% identical in my comparison.
* Their 1,417 shared lineage-exclusive families therefore appear as “doubletons,” although they may represent effectively the same biological genome.
* `GCF_000613185.1` and `GCF_005670675.1` differ by only 27 families and are also nearly sequence-identical.

Check BioSample, strain, and assembly provenance before treating these as independent observations. Functionally, your effective sample size looks much closer to about five major lineages than 15 independent strains.

## Strict cleaning removed an important BULB06 plasmid-like contig

This is probably the most interesting result.

Panaroo’s final matrix contains 93,647 CDS assignments, whereas `gene_data.csv` contains 96,938 records. Thus 3,291 CDSs were removed from the final graph.  

Most belonged to the outlier, but BULB06 lost 195 CDSs:

* five CDSs from its chromosome;
* all 190 CDSs from `BULB06 contig_2`.

That entire contig contains annotations consistent with a mobile, potentially conjugative secondary replicon:

* ESX/type VII secretion: `eccB`, `eccC`, `eccD`, `eccE`, `mycP`, WXG100 targets;
* conjugation: `traG`, `mobF`, VirB2/TrbC and VirB6/TrbL-family proteins;
* partitioning: `parA`, `parB`;
* mercury resistance: `merA`, `merB`, mercury transporters;
* numerous integrases and transposases;
* multiple toxin–antitoxin systems.

This is very unlikely to be ordinary annotation noise. Strict Panaroo appears to have removed it because it is a unique, internally connected contig without enough support elsewhere—not because it lacks biological coherence.

Consequently, the final matrix reports only three BULB06 singleton families while silently omitting a 190-CDS plasmid-like element. That is a severe downward bias if your scientific question includes plasmids, conjugation, resistance islands, or horizontal transfer.

## The eight isolates also share a second large mobile replicon

The 472 families fixed in all eight new isolates but absent from all public genomes are not randomly scattered:

* 201 occur on ANT01 `contig_2`, which contains 252 CDSs in total.
* The homologous 201-family block occurs on BULB06 `contig_3` plus `contig_4`, suggesting the same replicon was split across two assembly contigs.
* The remaining 271 occur on the chromosome.
* Approximately 77.5% of the 472 fixed-exclusive genes occur in uninterrupted runs of at least five genes.

ANT01 `contig_2` carries:

* `parA` and a ParB/RepB/Spo0J-family partition protein;
* conjugal-transfer and type IV secretion proteins;
* multiple integrases and transposases;
* several toxin–antitoxin systems;
* a putative `cas12m`;
* a terminal ESX/type VII secretion module including `eccB`, `eccCa`, `eccD`, `mycP`, and `eccE`.

That combination strongly supports a shared plasmid/megaplasmid or another stable mobile secondary replicon. In BULB06, the evidence suggests:

1. the shared replicon is fragmented into contigs 3 and 4; and
2. an additional, distinct 190-CDS conjugative plasmid-like contig was eliminated by strict cleaning.

That would reconcile the apparently homogeneous eight-isolate pangenome with the richer plasmid signals you have seen from geNomad.

## A second biological lead: a conserved NRPS–siderophore locus

Among the chromosome-associated new-cohort families is a particularly interesting block containing:

* an approximately 7,574-aa NRPS annotated `solG`;
* an MbtH-like protein;
* `mbtG`, encoding a lysine monooxygenase;
* an acyltransferase;
* two ABC transport components;
* ferric-siderophore uptake components;
* a putative siderophore-interacting protein.

The `solG` name should not be interpreted literally as proof of solanimycin production. In this actinobacterial context, the surrounding architecture is more suggestive of a large NRPS-dependent siderophore system. This locus is shared by all eight new isolates and absent as the same Panaroo family block from the public panel, making it a high-priority antiSMASH/domain-architecture target.

## Graph and structural outputs support lineage blocks

The final GML is internally consistent:

* 10,087 nodes, matching the final family matrix exactly;
* 10,599 edges;
* 79 connected components;
* one main 8,740-node component;
* only 38 isolated nodes;
* 76 of the 79 components consist entirely of cloud-frequency families.

Rare families are therefore usually arranged into long, internally connected blocks rather than appearing as isolated spurious gene calls. Several complete lineage-specific scaffolds form components of 245, 174, 170, 125, and 122 genes. 

The 1,812 rows in `struct_presence_absence.Rtab` should not be called 1,812 independent rearrangements. They are overlapping three-node graph paths or local structural haplotypes. Their dominant patterns again correspond to the major sampled lineages:

* 257 specific to the `GCF_039668…` pair;
* 149 specific to your eight isolates;
* 147 specific to the `GCA_000455185/000987455` pair;
* 116 specific to the `GCF_000613185/005670675` pair;
* 232 present in everything except the outlier. 

These overlapping triplets should be collapsed into structural loci before biological interpretation, especially because the fragmented public assemblies introduce contig-end effects.

## Refound genes are a minor but relevant phylogenetic caveat

Panaroo added 90 refound gene calls, of which 50 were marked pseudogene-like. Most occur in fragmented public genomes:

* `GCA_000455185.1`: 21 refound, 13 pseudo;
* `GCA_000987455.1`: 23 refound, 13 pseudo;
* the eight new genomes combined: only 15 refound.

Four universal-core families contain pseudo-refound sequences:

* `folB`;
* `folP`;
* `rsmH`;
* one CAAX-protease-family cluster.

I would exclude those four loci from a definitive concatenated phylogeny, since truncated refound sequences can generate misleading alignment columns.

## My recommended analysis design

Keep this strict run as a valuable diagnostic, but restructure the definitive work:

1. **Taxonomy-first run:** verify and probably remove `GCA_016197365.1`; inspect the two `GCF_039668…` accessions for duplication.

2. **Chromosomal/core run:** rerun Panaroo in strict mode using the taxonomically valid panel. You should obtain roughly 5,295 or more universal families rather than 1,466. Remove core loci containing pseudo-refound sequences and mask recombination before tree inference.

3. **Accessory/MGE run:** rerun with moderate or sensitive cleaning—or analyze plasmid-like contigs separately—so unique plasmids such as BULB06 `contig_2` are retained.

4. **Within-eight phylogeny:** use a core-SNP or reference-mapping approach with raw-read validation. With only 98 variable gene families, presence/absence alone will have limited resolution and will be sensitive to residual annotation or assembly differences.

5. **Plasmid comparison:** treat the shared ANT01-like secondary replicon and the unique BULB06 conjugative/mercury-resistance replicon as explicit objects rather than incidental pangenome families.

So my bottom line is: the run succeeded, but it has uncovered a dataset-QC and analysis-design issue rather than proving that *M. cosmeticum* has a 10,087-family, extremely open pangenome. Once the outlier, clonal redundancy, and strict-mode plasmid censorship are handled, the biology becomes much cleaner—and considerably more interesting.
